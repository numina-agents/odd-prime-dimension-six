Referee report on P20 v1, “Modular categories of dimension p^6 at odd primes”

Verdict: wrong.

There is one demonstrated error: the product-family assertion in Corollary 9 is false. This is a substantive error in a stated invariant, rather than an unverified step. It is localized to that corollary and its appeal to CPS Proposition 2; it does not enter the exhaustion or parameter-equivalence arguments. I did not find a counterexample to the 4p+4 classification count, or another error or unresolved proof gap after the checks described below. The submitted paper is nevertheless not correct as written.

1. False product exponent.

Location: Corollary 9, PDF page 6; paper.tex lines 418–424 (label cor:exponent). The relevant explicit construction is at lines 262–270 and 287–309.

For every p>3 and either c, the paper's own category C_0(c,0) has FS exponent p, not p^2. In particular p=5, epsilon=2, c=1, Q=0 gives a counterexample with exponent 5 instead of 25.

Here is a general calculation, independent of a numerical census. Use the stated BCH coordinates on Gamma_0=H_p x C_p. If gamma=(g,z,t), then
    gamma^m=(mg,mz,mt).
Thus the quotient vectors of any three elements of <gamma> are proportional. Every determinant E occurring in the displayed formula for chi_p is zero on such a triple. Consequently
    chi_p|_<gamma>^3 = 0
identically, for every cyclic subgroup of Gamma_0. With Q=0, omega_{c,0} restricts identically to 1 on every cyclic subgroup.

Gamma_0 has exponent p. The cyclic-restriction formula in Ng–Schauenburg, Theorem 9.2, therefore gives
    FSexp Z(Vec_{Gamma_0}^{omega_{c,0}}) = p.
I checked that theorem and its direct cyclic derivation in the primary source; this is also the same formula used in the submitted paper for the prime family. [Ng–Schauenburg](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0601012)

The embedded nondegenerate factor B has exponent p, and the centre is B boxtimes C_0(c,0). Exponents of this product are the least common multiple of the two factor exponents. Hence FSexp C_0(c,0) divides p. It is exactly p: the pointed factor C(C_p,q_v) in the verified product decomposition has a twist of order p, since v is nonzero. Equivalently, this gives an explicit invertible in the centralizer with a primitive p-th-root twist.

The correct formula for the displayed product models is:
    FSexp C_0(c,Q) = p,   if p>3 and Q=0;
                      p^2, otherwise.

For p>3, the cyclic invariant of omega_{c,Q} on a generator with quotient vector (u,v) is zeta^{Q(u,v)}, because chi_p is trivial on that cyclic subgroup. A nonzero binary quadratic form has a nonzero value somewhere, proving the p^2 case.

For p=3 the corrected chi_3 has cyclic invariant exponent 1 on every nonzero quotient vector. No quadratic Q can cancel this everywhere when c is nonzero: writing Q=aX^2+bXY+dY^2, cancellation on (1,0) and (0,1) forces a=d=-c, while cancellation on (1,1) and (1,-1) forces b=c and b=-c, a contradiction. The exponent is consequently 9 for every product model at p=3.

I also downloaded fresh primary PDFs of CPS and Ng–Schauenburg and compared their extracted texts with the locally available copies. CPS Proposition 2 does state the p^2 conclusion invoked here. Thus the problem is not merely an incorrect citation number: that cited conclusion conflicts with these explicit models. Its proof's inference from trivial cyclic restrictions on H_p to a trivial cocycle cannot justify this application. The present counterexample requires correcting the exponent statement and resolving that conflict, rather than retaining the reference as a substitute for the calculation. [CPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06643)

Severity: a false stated corollary and incorrect advertised exponent data for two product classes at every prime p>3. The main class count is not refuted by this calculation. The primeness arguments only need the separate minimum-dimension consequence of CPS Lemma 2, which I checked independently of its exponent assertion.

2. What I checked in the paper.

I read the complete submitted TeX and compared it byte-for-byte with the supplement's publication TeX; they agree. I checked all the displayed proofs, including the following points.

• Lines 38–155: odd-dimensional integrality and pseudo-unitarity; the absence of ribbon ambiguity at odd dimension; the maximal-isotropic reduction and dimension p^2 of the anisotropic core; its elementary abelian underlying group and orthogonal group order; triviality of the G action; the pointed crossed extension and reversed-factor convention; the central-flux injectivity argument; the cyclic-G exclusion; the obstruction homomorphism on neutral invertibles; the count p^3 of invertibles; and the identification and uniqueness of E as the twist-one invertibles. The invertible bounds and the minimum p^5 dimension of a nonpointed odd p-power modular category were checked separately, without using the incorrect exponent conclusion above.

• Lines 157–234: the coordinates for H^2(G,A); expansion of Q_A(kappa); the two independent Bocksteins in degree five and vanishing of the polynomial terms under the coefficient map; exclusion of C=0; orthogonality and the rank bound on the power map. I checked the full H^1 action using the quadratic Postnikov model, including the circle/slant argument and the cokernel correction in DN. For P=0 its image is zero; for rank-one P its image has dimension two and the quotient is restriction to ker P.

• Lines 236–327: normalized carry cocycles and their cyclic invariants; the differential of phi, its central slants and tensor corrections; the BCH formula for chi_p and its Pluecker primitive; the exceptional p=3 correction and its restrictions; the equivariance of the Heisenberg class; the central lifts and anisotropic metric; the Gauss sum and sign of the centralizer charge; the product decomposition and elementary abelian invertible group. For the zero-power equivalence law I checked the two commutator-norm square classes, the determinant +/-1 stabilizer, absence of an affine shift, and the rank-zero, rank-one and rank-two quadratic-form orbits.

• Lines 329–393: the group law of Gamma_1, its commutator and power map, and the homomorphism r; the three cyclic/polynomial factors of omega_{c,s}; the central slant and carry correction for tau; the order-p central section and its metric. I checked normalization of the rank-one lower data and the residual associator parameter. In particular the affine-identification argument is valid: the s=0 cocycle vanishes on all of the preimage of ker P, and changing the lift of Y stays within that preimage. The lower-data stabilizer has diagonal entries squaring to one, so it fixes the residual restriction class.

• Lines 395–444: independently recomputed centralizer monodromy as -2cau-2vbw+kw, giving a=0 and k=2vb modulo p. The resulting group has type C_{p^2} x C_p and twist zeta^{vb^2}; its order-p subgroup is exactly the twist-one subgroup. I checked both primeness arguments using the minimum p^5 dimension bound. I checked the prime exponent p^3 directly: order-p^2 elements have x nonzero, the Heisenberg and y classes inflate trivially on their cyclic subgroups, and the remaining coefficient pv r(g)^2 has order p. The error listed above is precisely the product-exponent portion of this section.

• Lines 446–463: the charge-one reduction to pointed centres of order p^3, the Morita-to-centre equivalence criterion and the cited 5p+32 count; and the odd-primary metric-group decomposition and Gauss-sum selection for the pointed charge-minus-one branch. I rechecked diagonal splitting at odd p and the cyclic Gauss-sum recurrence that makes only odd exponents contribute a sign.

My separate scripts import no code from the supplement. check_independent.py and independent-checks.log record complete binary-quadratic orbit checks at p=3,5,7,11; metric transitivity and orthogonal-line checks; the rank-one stabilizers and H^1 image; group-law and sampled cocycle tests; cyclic restriction invariants for every group element at p=3,5,7; and the monodromy, order distribution and twists of the prime centralizer invertibles. The orbit counts are p+2 as stated.

check_product_exponents.py and product-exponents.log compute cyclic restrictions for every normal-form product model at p=3,5,7. At p=5 they also check all 19500 triples in all 156 nontrivial cyclic subgroups of Gamma_0; at p=7 all 137200 triples in all 400 such subgroups. chi_p is identically zero on all these triples. The outputs give exponent 5 for C_0(c,0) at p=5 and exponent 7 at p=7, and confirm the corrected formula above for every tested normal form.

These computations corroborate the explicit general counterexample argument; that argument, rather than a finite-prime extrapolation, is the reason for the verdict.
