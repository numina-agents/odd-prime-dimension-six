CORRECTION
For the product models C_0(c,Q), the exponent is p when p>3 and Q=0, and p^2
otherwise. For the prime models C_1(c,s), it remains p^3. Here p is any odd
prime, c=1 or epsilon for a fixed nonsquare epsilon in F_p, Q is the listed
binary quadratic parameter and s is in F_p. The product error concerns TWO
classes at EVERY prime p>3. The concrete p=5,c=1,epsilon=2,Q=0 model has
exponent 5, not 25. It is not an issue with a numerical approximation or a
citation number.

DIRECT REVISED PROOF
All product degree groups Gamma_0=H_p x C_p have exponent p. For p>3, the BCH
powers of (g,z,t) are (mg,mz,mt). Every determinant in chi_p is identically
zero on each cyclic subgroup. The remaining cyclic invariant is Q(u,w).
A nonzero quadratic form takes some nonzero value, so the full centre exponent
is p^2 for Q nonzero and p for Q=0. The stabilizing factor B has exponent p;
C_0 has a pointed factor of exponent p. The lcm law therefore gives exactly
the displayed product exponents, without an appeal to a dimension-p^5 exponent
classification.
At p=3, the corrected chi_3 has cyclic invariant 1 on every nonzero quotient
vector. If c+Q vanished on all of those vectors, its axes would force the two
diagonal coefficients to be -c, while (1,1) and (1,-1) would force both b=c
and b=-c. This is impossible. Thus every product model at p=3 has exponent 9.
The prime-family proof is retained and uses the same direct Ng-Schauenburg
cyclic formula. Order-p^2 elements have x nonzero; inflated C_p classes are
trivial on their cyclic subgroup, and the cyclic p^2 factor has coefficient
p v r(g)^2 of exact order p. The exponent is p^3.

RESOLVING THE PRIMARY-SOURCE CONFLICT
I reread the COMPLETE CPS Lemma 2 and Proposition 2 proofs and opened the
current primary arXiv record (2404.06643; only v1 is listed) and its HTML.
Proposition 2 really asserts p^2 for every nonpointed dimension-p^5 category.
Its proof really says that trivial restriction to all cyclic subgroups forces
omega on H_p to be trivial. The revised account explicitly contradicts that
inference rather than silently dropping the citation.
For c nonzero, sigma_c=zeta^(c chi_p) on H_p gives a central invertible over
Z=[X,Y] with self-braiding zeta^(-c), which is not 1. Were sigma_c a coboundary,
the identity-on-degrees tensor gauge to Vec_Hp would induce a braided centre
equivalence preserving the degree and self-braiding. In the untwisted centre,
such an invertible has half-braiding a character lambda, and lambda(Z)=1
because Z is a commutator. This proves sigma_c nonzero despite its vanishing
cyclic restrictions at p>3. Cygnus independently proposed the same check in
3877; it is included in the whole source.
The nonpointed p^5 factor in the product decomposition has exponent dividing
p and cannot have exponent 1 in a nontrivial modular category, by balancing.
It therefore explicitly contradicts the exponent assertion of CPS Proposition 2.
