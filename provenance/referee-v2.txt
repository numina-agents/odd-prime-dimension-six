Referee report on P20 v2, “Modular categories of dimension p^6 at odd primes”

Verdict: correct.

The paper is correct. I checked the complete submitted text, the proofs and calculations, the relevant results in all thirteen cited primary sources, and the supplementary programs. I found no mathematical error or unresolved proof gap in version 2. The 4p+4 classification, the equivalence criteria, the prime/product distinction, the invertible groups, and the revised Frobenius–Schur exponents are supported by the arguments. This report concerns correctness only.

2. Proof audit, with locations in the submitted TeX.

• Lines 39–117, the initial reductions. I checked integrality at odd dimension, pseudo-unitarity and uniqueness of the ribbon structure. For the latter, the possible ribbon changes correspond to order-two invertibles; odd dimension excludes them. I checked the hypotheses of the nilpotent/maximal-isotropic reduction. The pointed anisotropic core has exponent p and rank at most two: a higher-exponent cyclic summand has an isotropic order-p subgroup, and an elementary quadratic space of rank greater than two is isotropic. Its dimension exponent is even because the ambient dimension is p^6; charge −1 excludes the trivial core. Thus its dimension is p^2 and a maximal Tannakian subcategory has dimension p^2. The anisotropic plane has orthogonal group of order 2(p+1), so the action of a group of order p^2 on it is trivial. GR then supplies the pointed full de-equivariantization and the stated central extension. I checked that the factor in the centre is the reversed core, with the sign used later.

I separately checked the dimension bounds used from CPS Lemma 2. They do not use CPS Proposition 2's exponent argument. In outline, a noninvertible's stabilizer among invertibles is nontrivial and lies in (C_ad)_pt. If that group had a unique order-p subgroup, that subgroup would fix all noninvertibles and, by the balancing equation and its Tannakian twist, centralize every simple, contradicting nondegeneracy. This gives the lower bound p^2. If the invertible group had size p^(n−1), a graded component would be too small for a noninvertible. If it had size p^(n−2), C_ad would be pointed of dimension p^2; its action on the unique noninvertible in a nonpointed graded component would force that noninvertible into C_pt, again a contradiction. Hence |Inv C|≤p^(n−3), and nonpointed odd p-power modular categories have dimension at least p^5.

• Lemma 2, lines 119–132. I checked the degree homomorphism, centrality of its image, and injectivity. An invertible lying over the tensor unit has self-braiding one, so anisotropy indeed kills the kernel. A group of order p^4 with this central subgroup of order p^2 has abelian quotient of order p^2 and therefore class at most two.

• Lemma 3, lines 134–159. A central extension of a cyclic group is abelian. In that case conjugation fixes all degrees, and projective irreducibles of the cyclic equivariantizing group have dimension one, giving the excluded pointed case. For G=C_p^2, I checked that tensor coherence makes a↦[alpha_a] a homomorphism to H^2(G,C^×)=C_p. Its kernel has at least p elements, each with p^2 invertible lifts. The upper bound p^3 forces equality and exhausts the invertibles. Their twists are the core twists. Anisotropy makes the twist-one objects exactly the p^2 lifts of the neutral unit. Every maximal Tannakian subcategory has dimension p^2 and is pointed, so it is precisely this same E. This verifies the preservation of E under unmarked braided equivalence; a generic Morita equivalence cannot create an additional identification here.

• Proposition 4, lines 161–198. I expanded Q_A(Ce+P_1y_1+P_2y_2), checked the two displayed Bocksteins, and checked the coefficient map to H^4(G,C^×). The polynomial terms die, while the two e y_i terms give independent classes. Their coefficients are B_A(C,P_i), as asserted. For C=0, all p^4 objects are fixed; the obstruction homomorphism then has a kernel of size at least p^3 and would give at least p^5 invertibles. Thus C≠0. Its orthogonal line is distinct from its own line by anisotropy, and the power map has rank zero or one.

• Proposition 5 and its consequences, lines 200–238. I checked the full H^1 identification, including the cokernel correction in DN. The identity-component Picard 3-type has the quadratic invariant q. On BG×S^1 a loop of the lower data is kappa+u lambda. Polarization, with u^2=0 and odd p, gives the stated slant B_A(kappa,lambda). This accounts for the full translation action, rather than just changes of associator by coboundaries. For zero power, e lambda=0. For kappa=Ce+Ty_1 the image is exactly the span of x_1y_1 and x_2y_1, so the remaining parameter is restriction to ker P. I also checked the three-dimensional binary-quadratic description of H^3(G,C^×).

• Lines 240–313, the product construction. I recomputed the carry-cocycle restrictions, the differential of phi, its central slant and tensor correction, and the determinant primitive defining chi_p for p>3. The displayed rational polynomial identities are valid in every allowed characteristic. At p=3, summing phi(g,g^j,g) over j gives −x^2y^2. Adding a_x+a_y yields 1 on each nonzero quotient vector and 0 at zero. The Heisenberg degree-four filtration in MMU makes the difference of classes with the same leading coefficient inflated; cyclic restrictions detect that inflated quadratic part. This verifies the exceptional GL_2 invariance, including central changes of lifts. For p>3 the chosen BCH formula has determinant weight two directly.

I checked the half-braidings, the group law of the central section, the anisotropic form −ca^2−vb^2, and the normalized Gauss sum −1. Splitting the two central metric lines gives exactly the product decomposition (8), including q_v on its last factor. The first factor has dimension p^5 and is nonpointed: a pointed centralizer would make the full Heisenberg centre pointed, although it contains nonpointed Rep(H_p). Its primeness follows from the independently checked minimum dimension. The central section and characters have exponent p, giving Inv C_0=C_p^3.

• Proposition 6, lines 315–331. I checked that the norm of the commutator has exactly two square-class orbits and that the stabilizer permits exactly determinant ±1 on G. The chosen Heisenberg class removes an affine shift, and the H^1 action is zero in this branch. For the resulting congruence action there is one rank-zero orbit, two rank-one orbits, and one rank-two orbit for each nonzero determinant. To check the rank-two normal form explicitly, choose a norm-one vector and extend it to a determinant-one basis; orthogonalizing the second vector preserves the basis determinant and yields X^2+dY^2 with the original determinant d. Thus the list has p+2 forms for each c.

• Lines 333–364, the prime construction. I checked associativity of the group law, its commutator and power relations, and the homomorphism r to C_(p^2). The cyclic factor in (9) has coefficient pv on that cyclic group. I recomputed the central slants of all three factors and the carry correction in (10). That correction makes the displayed central section elementary abelian of order p^2, even though its individual half-braiding cochains involve p^2-th roots. Its metric and the dimension, central charge and nonpointedness of the centralizer follow as claimed.

• Proposition 7, lines 366–397. I checked simultaneous normalization of the commutator and power vectors, not only their separate ranks. Orthogonality and changes of basis give the two c square classes and the displayed v. Modulo the H^1 image, restriction to K=ker P is the single residual parameter. The stabilizer has X↦aX+bY, Y↦dY with a^2=d^2=1. I checked the affine issue: the s=0 base cocycle is trivial on all of the preimage {x=0}, which is preserved by every lower-data equivalence. Any lift of Y remains in this preimage. Restriction to its cyclic section therefore detects the residual class without a translation, giving s'=d^2s=s. This proves exhaustion and distinctness of the 2p pairs.

• Proposition 8 and the conclusion of Theorem 1, lines 399–420 and 492–496. I independently computed monodromy with the embedded factor as zeta^(−2cau−2vbw+kw). Hence the centralizer condition is a=0 and k=2vb modulo p. The order and tensor calculation give C_(p^2)×C_p, with twists zeta^(vb^2). Precisely its subgroup of order-p elements has b=0, and all of those twists are one. The minimum-dimension bound forces any nontrivial modular factorization to contain a pointed factor of dimension p, which would require an order-p invertible of nontrivial twist. This proves primeness. The two branches are inequivalent by their invertible groups. The preceding reductions and orbit calculations therefore give exactly (2p+4)+2p=4p+4 classes.

• Corollary 9 and the cyclic-detection discussion, lines 422–490. I checked the corrected exponents directly using the cyclic-restriction formula, not CPS Proposition 2. For p>3, all quotient vectors in a cyclic subgroup of Gamma_0 are proportional, so every determinant in chi_p vanishes there identically. The remaining restriction is Q(u,w). Thus the full centre has exponent p for Q=0, and p^2 otherwise. At p=3, no binary quadratic form cancels the constant nonzero-vector invariant of c chi_3: the four test vectors in the proof impose the incompatible equations b=c and b=−c. The stabilizing factor has exponent p and the centralizer contains a pointed factor of exponent p, giving exactly the claimed centralizer exponents.

For Gamma_1, the order-p^2 elements are exactly those with x≠0. The Heisenberg and y terms inflate from C_p and are cohomologically trivial on these subgroups: the degree-two integral generator pulls back to p times the generator, so its square pulls back to zero in degree four. The remaining restriction has coefficient pv r(g)^2 of order p. These subgroups contribute p^3, and the others at most p^2. The stabilizing factor again cannot absorb the p^3 exponent.

The proof that the cyclically invisible Heisenberg class is nonzero is valid. A coboundary gauge preserves the degree and self-braiding of the central object over Z; in the untwisted centre every character is trivial on this commutator, contradicting its computed self-braiding zeta^(−c). CPS Proposition 2 really does make the inference being criticized. The submitted v2 explains its failure correctly and does not use that inference in its classification.

• Section 6, lines 498–515. I checked the charge-one reduction, the pointed Morita-to-centre criterion and MMU's 5p+32 count, including p=3. I also checked odd-primary diagonalization of metric groups, the determinant square-class invariant on each exponent block, and the cyclic Gauss-sum recurrence. Even-exponent blocks contribute 1; the odd-exponent blocks give precisely the stated product and selection rule.
